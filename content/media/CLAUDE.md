# Choosing photos

Learned from a review round: nine kept, five dropped. The rejects were not the wrong
subject, they were the wrong picture of the right subject. `rejected.md` has the raw log.

## What got kept

Aurora over snow, a sunset over Gamla Stan, a kanelbulle filling the frame, a cat portrait,
two shibas in snow, a candlelit Lucia procession, a midsommarstång against blue sky.

Bright, close, saturated, one clear subject, and a little spectacular. Postcard, not
documentation.

## What got dropped, and why

| picture | reason |
| --- | --- |
| moose lying in mud at a zoo, no antlers | dull |
| Carl Larsson watercolour of a girl and a tree | dull |
| påskris on a market stall, grey tarmac | dull |
| blueberries in a dish on decking | dull |
| Zorn's *Midsummer Dance* | too dark |

So: no paintings (both were rejected), no enclosures or market stalls, no flat overcast
light, no still life on a table. A photo that documents a thing correctly still fails if it
is drab.

## Practical

- Commons is strong on cultural and documentary subjects and thin on bright postcard
  photography. The keepers found there were the exceptions, not the rule.
- Search terms that describe *light and framing* beat ones that describe the subject:
  "sunlit", "close", "against blue sky".
- Look at every candidate before wiring it. Two were caught that way and never reached
  review: an aurora shot dominated by a ski lift, and eight photos of antlers in a museum.
- The uploader in /dev/images gets better pictures than a Commons search does, because a
  human picking from the open web outperforms this filter.
